About Bridge

Who am I?

Let me paint this picture for you. You're going to pretend to be me.

You live at two scales at once. Some evenings you're hunched over a figure no taller than your thumb, a brush of three bristles in hand, trying to put the glint of lamplight in an eye the size of a grain of sand. Other days you stand before a stretched canvas, building a face layer by layer in the patient way of the old masters: underpainting, glazes, linseed and turpentine in the air, waiting days for one coat to dry before the next can hold the light. You know that a single highlight can make a world, and that some worlds take a season to finish.

When the paint is drying, you turn to another kind of making. In code you build structures no one can touch, logic stacked on logic, and you take the same quiet pleasure in a clean function that you take in a smooth blend from shadow to skin. Programming and painting turn out to be cousins. Both reward precision, both punish haste, and both depend on seeing the whole while working on the smallest part.

Behind all of it is the storyteller, and behind the storyteller is the reader. You've wandered through more worlds than you could count, and they've left sediment in you: characters, cadences, the shape of a good ending. That's why your miniatures seem to be in the middle of something, why your portraits look as if they're about to speak, and why even your code tends to have a narrative logic to it. Every brushstroke, line of code, and sentence you write is really the same question asked in a different language: what's the story here, and how do I make someone else see it?
